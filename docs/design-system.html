Design system

The tokens and components behind the site, modeled on america.gov's How it works page and Apple's product pages: bold system sans headings, a neutral sans for reading, navy as the only accent, soft surfaces, large radii, and one soft shadow. Every rule lives in styles.css.

Principles

One accent.Navy marks the primary action and selected states. Everything else is ink, gray, or a 5% navy tint.
Serif for the brand, bold sans for headings.The logo and the "Hello" greeting use a semibold serif. Every other heading uses the bold system sans (SF Pro on Apple devices, Inter elsewhere). Body copy stays regular weight.
Soft, not boxed.Use filled surfaces and large radii instead of borders. Keep borders for dividers.
Lead with the point.Feature copy starts with a short bold sentence, then muted detail in the same line.
Never look official.Keep the "student project" notice. Don't use flags, seals, or .gov styling.
Accessible by default.Visible focus rings, 44px minimum targets, and no motion when the visitor has reduced motion turned on.

Color

Text and borders use alpha steps of ink, so they work on any surface. Dark mode swaps every token.

Showcase

For product stories, modeled on apple.com/apple-news: a gray band, a bold two-line statement in the system sans, then white cards that pair a short headline and gray copy with a phone cropped at the bottom. Screens show the assistant as a chat that messages first (scheduled roundups, deadline alerts as notifications, memory you can see), in the style of today's AI chat apps, without copying any company's logo or name. Alternate sides with .flip. Label any sample content "Sample" and never credit invented stories to a real outlet.
