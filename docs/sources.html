Trusted sources

Where Sol gets its facts.

Sol only uses the sources on this page. If none of them has the answer, Sol says so and points you to the office that would know.

How we pick a source

A source makes the list only if it passes one of three tests.

1

It runs the election.

Government offices that run elections or make the decisions, like the Secretary of State, your county recorder, and your city clerk.

2

It informs without endorsing.

Nonpartisan voter guides that explain races and measures and never back a candidate or party.

3

It reports with standards.

Newsrooms that cover Arizona government, publish corrections, and keep news separate from opinion.

Never a source: opinion pieces, endorsements, campaign sites, party sites, and social media posts, even from outlets on this list.

How Sol uses them

  • Dates and rules come from official offices only. Every deadline, voting rule, and registration answer links to an election office. If anything disagrees with an official office, the office wins.
  • Voter guides explain what's on your ballot. Sol uses them to describe an office or a measure, never for deadlines.
  • Newsrooms feed your roundup. Every story links to the original article, with local and state news first.
  • You can add your own outlets. They're used for news stories only, never for dates or voting rules.

What the list can't cover

  • Your exact districts. A ZIP code can fall in more than one district, so Sol never guesses. Arizona.Vote shows your own districts and sample ballot.
  • Small races. School boards and special districts get little news coverage. Sol points you to the Clean Elections guide and your county's sample ballot instead.
  • Some sites block AI tools. We removed The Arizona Republic and NPR because their sites block ChatGPT and Claude, so scheduled roundups couldn't read them.
  • Paywalls. A few outlets charge for some stories. Sol says when a story is paywalled and offers a free one on the same topic if it can find one.
  • Tribal elections. Tribal nations run their own elections, which Sol doesn't cover. For state and federal elections, tribal members vote through their county.
